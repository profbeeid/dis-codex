### M3 — The Blackout

The opening state was Love 48, Money 62, Control 56. Josh centralized the negotiating mandate. Dana converted the distributor’s demand into a time-limited championship pilot. Hugh identified skilled implementation teams—not cash—as the binding constraint. Horacio created bridge and emergency-relief paths while refusing an unverifiable political off-ramp. Gorman imposed evidence and sunset gates without taking over operations. Mara supported the bridge only if its economics and milestones became reviewable.

Jordan Lee accepted a fourteen-day bridge: temporary in-product ESPN access, a limited fee concession, Disney editorial authority, Disney custody of identifiable customer data, aggregated performance reporting, and a hard sunset. Disney funded viewer make-goods and a capped migration test. Direct migration was postponed after the capacity check showed it could not carry the affected base safely.

Terminal state: Love 48, Money 57, Control 58. Disney saved championship reach and core data rights. It accepted temporary distribution dependence and paid for rebates, bridge economics, and duplicated contingency work.

Five cards fired naturally: championship deadline, advertiser rebate demand, migration-capacity evidence, distributor bridge response, and public-blame segmentation. Repeated app-failure, generic backlash, and forced-settlement cards were unnecessary.

### M1 — The Empty Vault

Session 2 inherited the bridge costs, divided implementation capacity, board review, and unfinished direct alternative. Dana, Hugh, and Josh converged on two fully supported lanes: franchise scale as cash engine and original-owned worlds as renewal engine. They terminated the failed flagship, preserved Luma as a separable owned asset, and funded a staged Luma prototype with attachment and delivery gates. Creator-led interactive work remained alive only as capped experiments until implementation capacity returned.

Horacio protected chain of title and wind-down rights. Gorman required a common capital envelope and postmortem but did not select projects. Mara spent her white-paper lever to turn the portfolio decision into enforceable capital-allocation review. Avery’s cooperation became conditional on creator credit and structural protection, not flattering promises.

Terminal state: Love 50, Money 59, Control 60. The synthesis worked because the room killed a project without killing its strongest learning. This was not free: talent trust weakened, interactive expansion slowed, and both surviving portfolios now carry proof burdens.

Six cards fired naturally: capacity ledger, flagship overrun/failure, segmented Luma evidence, franchise demand evidence, talent-rights response, and a creator-interactive partner option. Meme revival, arbitrary rival poaching, and repeated Luma evidence added color but not decisions.

## Council dynamics

M3’s strongest conversion was: spend Money on rebates and a bridge to preserve Love, while refusing permanent surrender of data and editorial Control. The uncomfortable truth is that Disney preserved contractual Control while still renting practical access.

M1’s strongest conversion was: franchise Money finances owned-original Love and Control, while the failed flagship is killed to cap cash burn. The Luma carve-out made the sacrifice intelligent rather than indiscriminate.

The room did not naturally produce a coup or a deep Dana–Josh split. That is useful. Forcing either would turn governance into theater. The natural conflict was subtler: Mara and Gorman converted executive discretion into milestones, while Josh protected the boundary between accountability and board management.

## Provisional ending

The earned ending is **costly independence with a rented-access bridge**. Disney learned to stage uncertainty, preserve rights, and kill whole projects without discarding tested assets. It did not solve distribution dependence or implementation scarcity.

Three unpaid invoices enter later sessions:

1. The championship bridge expires before Disney has built a credible direct alternative.
2. Rebate and bridge costs compete with the two supported portfolio lanes for the same managerial attention.
3. Luma, franchise renewal, and creator trust must now prove themselves; failure turns Mara’s review into a governance campaign.

## What to change before September

Keep the opening crises and the segmented-evidence cards. Remove duplicate bad-news cards. Add a directive-consolidation rule for the 21-seat room: one lead author, named co-sponsors, one implementation owner, and one consolidated revision. Put the capacity ledger on the dais table before M1. The likely failure is no longer “the room runs out of plot.” It is “the room generates more paper than the dais can convert into consequences.”
